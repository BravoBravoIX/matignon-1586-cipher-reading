# BnF fr. 15573 ff. 379r–381v: French text as produced by the key (partial reading)

Despatch in Matignon's Cipher-3, autumn 1586 (sender probably Matignon; addressed to the King).
Images: Gallica `btv1b90618780` views 740–745, Source gallica.bnf.fr / BnF.

**Status: partial reading (v0.2), work in progress.** 2,837 signs were read by Claude Opus 5.5 against a sign sheet:

- five passes, voted, with 25 image-checked corrections;
- unresolved stretches re-read at a larger scale.

The decoder runs a whole page at a time. It may use another pass's name for a sign at a cost; every such use is recorded in the kit's decode.json under `switches`.

**How the text was made:**

- The letters come only from the key (`data/key-cipher3.json`): Tomokiyo's table plus values aligned on the 1586 decipherment of a sister letter.
- Where a sign has two values (y = t or n; 8 = v or o; d = f or, by conjecture, *vostre*), the word decoder chose one with the period French lexicon. The choice is recorded in `data/<page>/key.json`.

**How to read the two versions:**

- **Literal** is the key's output with no spaces; · marks a sign with no value.
- **Normalised** has only spaces added by code. A stretch in [brackets] matched no dictionary word, so expect reader errors there. A word that runs on to the next line ends in =. No letter is changed.
- Reader accuracy on known text was about 0.93–0.96 per sign, so expect roughly one sign in twenty to be wrong.
